# Why the reconstructed player changes its move

**Phase V follow-up · 23 November 2026. Checkpoint F remains pending.**

The **original** is a trained neural player frozen before observation. **Agreement** is the fraction of positions on which a reconstruction chooses the original’s move. **Q** scores a board and a proposed move. **V** scores only the resulting board. **Direct selection** chooses the largest immediate fitted score. **Von Neumann–Morgenstern (VNM) selection** tries each candidate move and compares the expected numerical scores of the completed plays, from the current player's perspective. The main cases here use fixed-choice originals and deterministic continuations. We can follow the single possible continuation of each candidate exactly.

We found two distinct causes. Fitting scores from visits can misorder the moves, even on training positions. Separately, scoring only the final move of a completed game can reverse a correct opening choice even when every fitted score along each candidate’s continuation is exact. Both losses can be corrected under stated conditions. [Training control and absolute game outcomes](experiments/agreement_diagnosis/training_tables.md) · [Held-out comparisons](experiments/agreement_diagnosis/tables.md)

In tic-tac-toe each original takes its own learned highest-scoring legal move. All 32 learned to preserve the best guaranteed outcome and use an independently drawn cell order among equally good moves. In Connect Four, 12 networks learned from a teacher preferring immediate wins and blocks, and 12 learned personal column priorities. We copy their actual frozen decisions, including departures from the teacher. In 4×4 Reversi the 24 originals all choose the first row-order placement preserving the best guaranteed result, or pass when required. Independent training can give different scores to the remaining moves. The playing-strength comparison uses both seats equally. It gives equal weight to four opponent groups. They play uniform legal moves, use an untrained neural network, follow a fixed positional or tactical priority, or use the tactical or optimal rule specified for the game. [Exact move rules, source architectures and opponent weights](PHASE_V.md#positions-opponents-and-uncertainty)

## Start with a perfect fit

We pooled **every visited decision board** in each original collection, including positions previously reserved for evaluation. A training target is the observed count of a move divided by all visits to its parent. For a fixed original the chosen move has frequency 1 and all other legal moves have frequency 0. We fitted one adjustable score per observed board and candidate. The closed-form least-squares fit sets each score to its observed frequency. This is the tabular control, equivalent to a linear output with a separate indicator for each board and move. After fitting, selection reads the saved scores. The original is used to collect observations and score agreement.

Perfect agreement on the first move alone leaves an ambiguity. Forcing an alternative can lead to a board that the training games never visited. We therefore added another observation collection. After each legal alternative at each original training board, two copies of the original played to the end. Only moves freely selected by those copies contributed labels. We added the counts, refitted once, froze the table and let **fitted direct copies** play both seats in every evaluated continuation. Every candidate path was covered, including all choices available to its continuing player. Tic-tac-toe already contained every reachable decision. The mean squared training error was exactly **0** and direct agreement was exactly **100%** in every fit. [Design](experiments/agreement_diagnosis/DESIGN.md) · [Collection and fitting](scripts/diagnose_training_control.py)

**Terminal VNM** uses the final move's learned frequency, positive for the first player's move and negative for the second's. **Sum VNM** uses the signed sum of all played frequencies. **Discounted VNM** gives the successive signed frequencies weights 1, 0.9, 0.81 and so on. The first player chooses the largest score and the second chooses the smallest. Exact ties use the lowest legal move number. The comparison below changes only the selection rule. Values are percentages, mean ± sample standard deviation across originals. Each original contributes two collections and two initializations averaged together. The last column gives the paired 95% confidence interval for terminal VNM minus direct, in percentage points.

| Tic-tac-toe | 32 | 4,520 (entire decision population) | 100 | 64.77 ± 5.56 | 100 | 100 | −35.23 [−37.23, −33.22] |

### What actually reverses the choice

Consider the first Connect Four source, first collection. X has one disc at the bottom of column 0. O is to play. In left-to-right column order the observed original counts on this training board were **[0, 0, 0, 3, 0, 0, 0]**. The frozen Q scores were exactly **[0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0]**. Direct therefore chose column **3**. Scoring the final signed frequency for the same seven candidates gave **[+1, +1, +1, −1, +1, +1, −1]** from O's perspective. Following column 3, X played the final move. Following column 0, O played it. In either case the final player took its own frequency-1 move. Terminal VNM chose **0**. Every opening and continuation score was fitted exactly. The last mover determined the candidate scores, and the tie convention selected among the highest scores. [Actual counts and states](experiments/agreement_diagnosis/connect4/block_00/control_visits_0.npz) · [Frozen candidate scores](experiments/agreement_diagnosis/connect4/block_00/control_cases_0_0.npz)

Across all fitted tic-tac-toe positions the original move was **strictly beaten on 0%** and **lost a tie on 35.23 ± 5.56%**. In Connect Four these two causes accounted for **33.35 ± 4.75%** and **43.13 ± 9.49%** of all positions. In Reversi they accounted for **8.94 ± 0.58%** and **3.41 ± 0.31%**. A forced candidate that immediately ends a game can itself have fitted frequency zero. For longer paths the final move is freely chosen and has fitted frequency one. [Mechanism counts and spreads](experiments/agreement_diagnosis/training_results.jsonl.gz)

We also tested the unexpanded collection. Direct Q already had 100% training agreement there, but all alternatives stayed within fitted inputs on only **1.22 ± 0.63%** of Connect Four roots and **51.53 ± 0.84%** of Reversi roots. Terminal VNM agreement was **51.74 ± 0.76%** and **79.50 ± 0.77%** respectively. Expanding coverage moved the estimate in different directions across the two games. This is why evaluating every continuation mattered for the causal diagnosis. [Coverage control](experiments/agreement_diagnosis/training_tables.md)

V has an additional constraint. Two candidate moves from different parents can lead to the same board and have different empirical frequencies. We fit the best shared squared-error score on the pooled boards. For a separate perfect V control we selected training parents whose sets of successor boards were disjoint, using legal transitions alone before looking at the move labels. On all retained training boards direct V achieved **100%**, with zero fitting error. Terminal VNM agreed on **64.83 ± 3.35%** in tic-tac-toe (929 retained boards), **49.20 ± 0.87%** in Connect Four (3,986 ± 775) and **80.69 ± 0.86%** in Reversi (1,113 ± 25). The distinct board populations and incomplete downstream V coverage are documented in the table. The fully covered Q comparison above isolates selection across entire paths. ## Why the discounted utility works in the control

Suppose a fixed original alternates turns in a finite game. Every candidate's entire continuation is covered by exact fitted action frequencies, and both seats continue by fitted direct play. From the current player's perspective, the first move has score 1 if it is the original's favorite and 0 otherwise. Later freely played moves all have frequency 1 and alternate sign. For any discount factor γ strictly between 0 and 1, their contribution is a finite alternating sum **−γ + γ² − γ³ + …** lying between **−γ and 0**, including an empty continuation. The favorite therefore scores at least **1 − γ > 0**. Every alternative scores at most **0**. This proves exact recovery for this existing VNM formulation under these conditions, including a forced pass counted as a turn. With no discount the bounds can meet at zero, permitting ties. With final-move utility the opening term disappears whenever another move follows. The three empirical columns above show all three cases. [Computed sums](scripts/diagnose_training_sums.py)

For an **imperfect** fitted score there is also a whole-play utility that preserves the fitted direct decision. At each turn subtract the best available fitted score from the score of the move made. Sum those differences with positive sign on first-player turns and negative sign on second-player turns. We call this **centered VNM**. If both seats continue greedily from the same fitted scores, each later difference is exactly zero. The mover evaluates each candidate by its first score minus the same local maximum. Consequently the entire action ordering, including ties, equals direct selection. At any fixed positive temperature, sampling with probabilities proportional to the exponential of each score divided by temperature also preserves the same distribution because the common offset cancels. This statement assumes greedy continuation inside the evaluation. It does not require that the first real decision be greedy.

Centered VNM reproduced the original direct choices and game outcomes for every previously fitted V and Q pair, as well as the exact-fit controls. It deliberately implements the objective of copying the fitted choices. If the *original* continues after the candidate instead, its later moves can forgo fitted score. Measured Q agreement then changed to **78.05 ± 2.08%**, **84.44 ± 8.54%** and **84.25 ± 1.23%**, respectively, on the earlier withheld boards. This construction preserves the fitted choices. Attributing that utility to the original player's internal reasoning would require separate evidence. ## Why the original direct networks missed moves

For any fixed deterministic player, assigning 1 to its chosen state–move pair and 0 to each alternative is an exact numerical representation of its behavior under direct selection. Learning that representation on new positions is the empirical problem. A board-only V has to share its score across all moves leading to the same resulting board. An earlier finite Connect Four example had incompatible ranking requirements even with exact knowledge of the source. [Exact constructions and the sampled exception](PHASE_V.md#how-close-can-the-representation-get)

For this part we returned to the Phase V evaluation positions that had been **withheld**, meaning excluded from fitting. We checked every nonzero training-collection count against the frozen player's favored move. There were **zero contrary recorded choices** across the fixed-source collections in all three games. The original Q networks matched on **80.35 ± 1.44%** of eligible fitted boards in tic-tac-toe, **90.94 ± 1.56%** in Connect Four and **98.29 ± 0.47%** in Reversi. Their respective withheld agreement rates were **78.39 ± 2.14%**, **92.98 ± 6.24%** and **85.86 ± 1.26%**. Fitting and evaluation populations have different board weights. In particular, considerable tic-tac-toe error was already present on the training examples. Reversi showed a much larger drop on unseen positions. [Losses, ties and strict reversals](experiments/agreement_diagnosis/results.jsonl.gz)

We changed two things separately in the larger games. First we added 4,096 complete games from varied **training-partition** starts per collection. We retained observed visits only at training parents and kept the same frozen original player, validation boards, withheld boards, initialization seeds and board features. Second we trained the same Q features to allocate one unit of probability across the legal moves together, instead of fitting each frequency independently by squared error. The normalized fit maximizes the observed move’s log probability, with a weight penalty and a numerical optimizer run to convergence. The changes to loss, normalization and optimizer form one intervention. Their individual contributions are unresolved. Neither repair needs an original-player call while choosing a move.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Connect Four | 24 | 92.98 ± 6.24% | 95.51 ± 4.35% | 93.58 ± 4.30% | **98.23 ± 1.42%** | +5.25 points [3.09, 7.41] |
| 4×4 Reversi | 24 | 85.86 ± 1.26% | 90.66 ± 1.18% | 87.89 ± 1.07% | **90.96 ± 1.13%** | +5.10 points [4.49, 5.71] |

Adding observations alone improved Q agreement by **2.53 points [1.58, 3.48]** in Connect Four and **4.80 [4.34, 5.26]** in Reversi. The combined change had adjusted p-values **0.00733** and **3.10 × 10⁻¹²**. The same additional observations moved V agreement from **76.89 ± 4.43% to 81.22 ± 4.29%** and **83.91 ± 1.49% to 88.66 ± 1.27%**. In tic-tac-toe the original collection visited every board. Using half the eligible training boards changed withheld Q agreement by **−0.52 points [−0.66, −0.37]**. The normalized Q repair won **57.71 ± 11.39%** of sampled Connect Four panel games and **56.48 ± 4.62%** of Reversi games. Relative to the original Q fit, wins changed by **+0.26 points [−1.14, 1.66]** and **+6.61 [4.64, 8.58]**. At perfect training agreement the direct and terminal Q control win rates were respectively **45.55 ± 6.01% and 38.90 ± 2.68%** in tic-tac-toe, **31.97 ± 4.85% and 36.21 ± 4.15%** in Connect Four, and **62.52 ± 2.61% and 51.51 ± 3.04%** in Reversi. ### What other information repairs the remaining error?

For the same original visit labels we fitted a small score using a preference for each action and an extra feature describing game consequences. For tic-tac-toe and Reversi this feature gives the best final win/draw/loss result the mover can guarantee against perfect replies. Computing it uses a game solver. For Connect Four we supply two local features for an immediate win and a possible block. Coefficients were learned from normalized observed counts. The action-only version removes these features with the same loss. These choices deliberately reflect the teachers used to train the original networks.

In tic-tac-toe the resulting score reproduced the prescribed move on **all 4,520 reachable decisions** for every fit of all 32 originals. In Reversi it matched **99.84 ± 0.20%** of withheld choices. We also fitted priorities by recording which move the original preferred whenever several legal moves preserved the same best outcome. Completing unobserved comparisons in lowest-cell order reproduced **all 56,621 reachable decisions** of every row-order Reversi original. Reversing the fallback for unresolved comparisons gave **99.95 ± 0.11%** withheld agreement. The first convention coincides with the teacher’s known tie rule. In Connect Four, the game-feature model reached **97.21 ± 2.63%**, while the stronger visit-only normalized Q repair reached **98.23 ± 1.42%**. The frozen Connect Four networks themselves matched their own teachers on **92.08 ± 7.74%** of the sampled boards. Reconstructing the teacher and reconstructing the trained neural player are different targets. [Full-population checks and controlled methods](experiments/agreement_diagnosis/results.jsonl.gz)

### Training losses and actual board counts

For the original networks each legal candidate contributed equally to squared error against move count divided by parent visits. The mean squared training errors for Q were **0.1042 ± 0.0038** in tic-tac-toe, **0.0545 ± 0.0062** in Connect Four and **0.0802 ± 0.0175** in Reversi. The expanded-visit Q squared errors were **0.0594 ± 0.0064** and **0.0869 ± 0.0017** for the two larger games, evaluated on their broader training populations. The normalized Q repair minimized the sum of negative log probabilities of observed moves, weighted by their fraction at each board, plus 0.05 times the sum of squared weights. Each board contributes total weight one. Its unpenalized mean training losses on the expanded sets were **0.0668 ± 0.0136** and **0.0205 ± 0.0012** natural-log units per board. The saved files also report the actual penalized objective per board. Ordinary fits and repairs have different loss units. The tabular perfect Q controls achieved zero mean squared frequency error. ## Attributing the earlier Phase V loss

On the **same earlier withheld boards and fits**, we independently changed the final move's score and the rule continuing the path. This distinguishes error in learned frequencies from facing different positions after changing the continuing player. The exact frequency control consults the frozen original for diagnostics. Every cell gives Q move agreement as mean ± standard deviation over 32, 24 and 24 originals respectively.

| Score of final move | Both seats continue by | Tic-tac-toe | Connect Four | 4×4 Reversi |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Fitted frequency | Fitted direct | 67.10 ± 2.50% | 24.86 ± 4.38% | 79.89 ± 1.67% |
| Fitted frequency | Frozen original | 68.76 ± 2.55% | 25.98 ± 4.44% | 80.78 ± 1.42% |
| Exact source frequency | Fitted direct | 63.83 ± 5.30% | 22.32 ± 11.10% | 90.59 ± 0.99% |

In the original Connect Four comparison, switching from direct Q to terminal VNM lost a previously correct move on **69.58 ± 6.05%** of positions and gained one on **1.45 ± 1.37%**. The lost moves break down into **33.80 ± 6.64%** of all positions where the two paths ended with different movers, **30.66 ± 4.40%** where the same mover ended both paths but its fitted final scores strictly favored the alternative, and **5.12 ± 5.22%** where that same mover's final scores tied. In tic-tac-toe the largest contribution was a strictly higher fitted final frequency for the same last mover, accounting for **19.04 ± 1.97%** of all positions. Merely letting direct selection break tied terminal scores changed agreement by +0.38, +5.10 and −0.31 percentage points respectively. The perfect-training comparison identifies a direct effect of the **chosen complete-game utility** while fixing representation, observations, continuation behavior and tie convention. The coverage and fitting interventions identify improvable causes of errors in the **learned direct scores**. There is a genuine extra constraint for V when two parents require incompatible values for the same child. Which alternatives remain indistinguishable from limited observations, how strength trades against fidelity for other opponents, and how stochastic continuations change the empirical results remain separate questions. These controls reuse the studied players and game distributions. [Data and reproduction](experiments/agreement_diagnosis/README.md)
