# What the project takes from the literature

A **policy** gives a move or probabilities for the available moves. A **decoder** converts scores into choices. A **return** is the payoff accumulated under specified rules of play. The numbered families PV-H1–PV-H7 organize claims about representation, ambiguity, repeated fits, explanation, sampling, transfer across domains and neural mechanisms. The references below identify which method or result carries over and where this project owes a proof or a measurement. The author's motivation for proposing a correspondence between policies and values is also recorded in the README. [Claim ledger](CLAIM_LEDGER.md) · [Origin of the conjecture](README.md)

## Decisions, payoffs and observations

| Primary source | What carries over | Boundary in this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Howard, *Dynamic Programming and Markov Processes* (1960), value determination and policy improvement](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1960-howard-dynamicprogrammingmarkovprocesses.pdf) | Evaluate what happens when a policy continues, then compare actions using those returns. | Selecting the best first move can change a source policy. Our alternating-player signs, terminal points and enumeration need independent checks. H1e. |
| [von Neumann and Morgenstern, *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior*, chapter I, §3](https://press.princeton.edu/books/ebook/9781400829460/theory-of-games-and-economic-behavior) | Under preference axioms on lotteries (probability distributions over consequences), a numerical utility can be averaged with the outcome probabilities. Under the representation's assumptions its units admit positive affine changes. | VNM means von Neumann–Morgenstern expected utility. Declaring a learned utility of a whole play gives a model to evaluate. Move counts alone do not establish the lottery preference axioms. H1e, H2d, H4a. |
| [Sen, *Choice Functions and Revealed Preference* (1971)](https://sen.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/choice-functions-and-revealed-preference) | Ask which restrictions on choices as menus change permit rationalization by a preference relation. | Our finite pair-query recovery, access costs and examples of reversals require explicit assumptions about ties and actual source responses. H2c, H4a. |
| [McFadden, *Conditional Logit Analysis of Qualitative Choice Behavior* (1974)](https://eml.berkeley.edu/reprints/mcfadden/zarembka.pdf) | Exponentiated systematic scores normalized across alternatives give a choice distribution under a specified random-utility model. | We can use exponential sampling as a decoder. The claim that actual source variability follows that model needs observations. Temperature, base weights, forbidden actions and tied maxima require local treatment. H1b, H2b, H5. |
| [Ng and Russell, *Algorithms for Inverse Reinforcement Learning* (2000)](https://ai.stanford.edu/~ang/papers/icml00-irl.pdf) | Ask which rewards make observed behavior optimal under known decision dynamics. Degenerate or alternative rewards motivate extra structure. | Recovering a short utility with predictive power on unobserved choices is a stronger empirical target than exhibiting one compatible score. H2d. |
| [Ziebart et al., *Maximum Entropy Inverse Reinforcement Learning* (2008)](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2008/AAAI08-227.pdf) | Model distributions over paths with rewards expressed in shared path features. Entropy measures spread of probability across alternatives. | A globally normalized path model, sampling an immediate move score and averaging utility over a specified continuation define different distributions. Compare under the same access. H2d, H5. |
| [Ng, Harada and Russell, *Policy Invariance under Reward Transformations* (1999)](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pabbeel/cs287-fa09/readings/NgHaradaRussell-shaping-ICML1999.pdf) | Adding a discounted difference of a state potential to reward preserves optimal policies under the paper's assumptions. | Terminal conditions and the discount matter. Our equivalence classes identify scores that give the same declared responses and can differ by decoder. H2a–b. |
| [Skalse et al., *Invariance in Policy Optimisation and Partial Identifiability in Reward Learning* (2023)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/skalse23a.html) | Characterize reward transformations invisible to particular data sources and consequential for downstream tasks. | Record the query family and intended use. Which board-score differences survive a constrained query or extra rollout is a project question. H2a–d. |
| [Grimm et al., *The Value Equivalence Principle for Model-Based Reinforcement Learning* (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03506) | Compare transition models by their effect on updates of selected value functions under selected policies. | This motivates equivalence relative to a consumer. Our objects are representations of choices, so equality of their decoded responses needs its own theorem. H2a. |

## Imitation, explanation and mechanisms

| Primary source | What carries over | Boundary in this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Rusu et al., *Policy Distillation* (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06295) | Train a smaller network to reproduce information supplied by a trained player. | A teacher's scores can supply information unavailable from one chosen move. Match access and count observations when comparing imitation and utility recovery. H1, H3, H4, H6. |
| [Ross, Gordon and Bagnell, *A Reduction of Imitation Learning and Structured Prediction to No-Regret Online Learning* (2011)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html) | A learner's own actions alter the states it will encounter. Querying an expert on those states addresses that change in training distribution. | Report errors on independently held-out states and performance during full deployment. Our random legal starts and rare-state sampling define their own populations. H3, H6. |
| [Silver et al., *Mastering Chess and Shogi by Self-Play with a General Reinforcement Learning Algorithm* (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01815) | A game study can distinguish move probabilities, predicted game outcome and move selection by search. | The original network is frozen before we fit observed transitions. AlphaZero trains with search and self-play and supplies game precedent for these distinctions. H1e, H6. |
| [Bastani, Pu and Solar-Lezama, *Verifiable Reinforcement Learning via Policy Extraction* (2018)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/e6d8545daa42d5ced125a4bf747b3688-Abstract.html) | Extract a decision tree, a sequence of explicit branching rules, and evaluate its behavior. Their extraction uses an expert and its action values. | Compare an explanation's size and predictive error at matched access. Human usefulness needs a user study. H4a–b. |
| [Kornblith et al., *Similarity of Neural Network Representations Revisited* (2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/kornblith19a.html) | Compare the pattern of responses across examples, with attention to which transformations a similarity statistic ignores. | Agreement of two hidden representations across inputs provides evidence about association. Stable value geometry and causal influence require separate measurements. H3c, H7a. |
| [Geiger et al., *Causal Abstractions of Neural Networks* (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02997) | Interchange interventions substitute internal states between examples and test the resulting behavior against a proposed causal model. | We must specify which external score to align, held-out interventions and matched controls. Evidence for a mechanism is specific to a model and intervention. H7b–c. |

## Logical readouts and training history

- McCulloch and Pitts constructed logical operations with threshold units. Zadeh defined fuzzy membership as a degree in the unit interval. Badreddine and colleagues gave formulas graded truth values in a differentiable logic system. These supply concrete interpretations of numerical computation. Their different meanings for a number in [0,1] matter when applying the analogy to game utility. [McCulloch and Pitts (1943)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02478259) · [Zadeh (1965)](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pister/publications/FamousPapers/Zadeh65FuzzySets.pdf) · [Badreddine et al., *Logic Tensor Networks*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13635)
- Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams propagated derivatives of output error to change weights and learn internal representations. Nair and Hinton studied rectified units, which respond to positive inputs, in a particular class of probabilistic networks. These papers document specific methods. Whether bounded versus unbounded intermediate scores improve transfer or interpretation requires controlling the architecture, objective and training budget. Negating the training loss gives a score on parameters whose maximizers minimize that loss. H7c concerns the further empirical interpretation. [Error propagation (1986)](https://www.nature.com/articles/323533a0) · [Rectified units (2010)](https://icml.cc/2010/papers/432.pdf)

## Obligations retained by the project

| Claim family | Proof or evidence still owed here |
| --- | --- |
| H1 · Representation | Give the finite construction, exact treatment of zero probability, and distance construction for continuous actions. Determine when merging histories into one resulting board preserves choices. Measure return-based imitation with a specified continuation. |
| H2 · Identifiability | Prove the project-specific equivalence and normalization statements, including available menus and ties. Compare compact utilities inferred from choices on fresh queries. |
| H3 · Stability | Estimate variation over independent datasets, initial weights and continuation draws after aligning units. The cited similarity and imitation studies do not measure these game-specific effects. |
| H4 · Interpretation | Predict fresh source choices under changed menus and outcome probabilities. Measure predictive gain, size and stability at comparable access. |
| H5 · Sampling | Derive the limit and invariances for the chosen base weights and temperature. Measure the change in move diversity, state coverage and return. |
| H6 · Common interface | Demonstrate the same declarations on games, continuous actions and sequence prefixes, keeping candidate coverage and computational cost explicit. |
| H7 · Mechanism | Compare prediction from internals, targeted changes and controls under a changed objective. The historical progression motivates a comparison, whose causal direction remains an open question. |

**Self-review.** Policy improvement and reward ambiguity offer limiting examples. Distillation, structured preference models and controlled interventions offer constructive comparisons. The audit assigns each transfer a specific use and reserves original claims for the project's proofs or measurements.
