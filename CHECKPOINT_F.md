# Checkpoint F · What the three-game benchmark establishes

23 November 2026. **Release the Phase V empirical report and its reproducible board-game benchmark together, before extending the study to other domains.** A player reconstructed from observed moves can win more games than the original while choosing different moves. Connect Four shows this most clearly. Tic-tac-toe shows the cost of pursuing wins against fallible opponents, and 4×4 Reversi shows how often a forced move can conceal disagreement. Task 83 is the next unopened item. [Empirical report](PHASE_V.md) · [Roadmap](TODO.md#resume-here)

## What was compared

The **original** is the trained neural player whose weights were frozen before observation. Each tic-tac-toe original learned to take a move that preserves its best guaranteed outcome against perfect replies, using its personal priority among equally good cells. In Connect Four, half learned a teacher that scores two for winning immediately and one for occupying a column where the opponent could immediately win, with a personal order for ties. Half learned a personal column order alone. We scored each network's actual choices. In 4×4 Reversi, all originals choose the first move in row order preserving their best guaranteed outcome, or pass when required. The weights and lower-ranked moves can vary between these networks. [Original training, architectures and checks](PHASE_V.md#what-the-players-learned)

A **board-scoring network (V)** estimates how often the original takes a move, given the board after that move. A **board-and-move network (Q)** sees the preceding board and the candidate move. **Direct selection** chooses the legal move with the highest fitted frequency. **Von Neumann–Morgenstern (VNM) selection** tries every legal move and chooses the one with the highest expected score of a completed game. The project standard uses V. The Q comparison changes the network input and keeps the selection rule. [Frozen selection rule](experiments/win_selection/standard.json)

For standard VNM, the player choosing now earns 1 for a win, ¼ for a draw and 0 for a loss. Add 0.02 times the fitted frequency of each simulated move, positive for its own turns and negative for its opponent's, multiplying by 0.9 at each successive turn. On later turns it takes an immediate win, avoids an immediate loss and minimizes immediate winning replies. In Reversi it next prefers a corner, fewer opposing corner moves, then fewer opposing placements. Fitted frequency breaks remaining ties, followed by move number. The simulated opponent follows one of four rules, drawn with equal probability once per game. It can use direct, the same guarded rule, a positional preference, or uniform legal play. The positional player prefers center then corners in tic-tac-toe, central columns in Connect Four, and corners then edges in Reversi. Fixed ties go to the lowest move number. Tic-tac-toe sums all uniform-reply probabilities exactly. Each larger game uses 16 sampled paths for the uniform quarter and one for each other opponent. Actual play chooses the highest expectation, breaks ties by lowest move number and plans again after each reply. The planner has the game rules and fitted scores. It has no access to the original or the identity of its actual opponent. [Implementation and selection design](experiments/win_selection/DESIGN.md)

**Win rate** counts wins from both seats against independent external opponents. Four groups each receive one quarter of the weight. One plays uniformly. One uses the highest-scoring legal move of a fresh, untrained neural network. In tic-tac-toe the other two are a win/block/center/corner player and a player preserving the best guaranteed result. In Connect Four they prefer central columns, or immediate wins and blocks followed by centrality. In Reversi they prefer corners then edges, or preserve the best guaranteed result with row-order ties. The games against an exact copy are a separate measurement. **Move agreement** is the weighted fraction of withheld positions on which the reconstruction picks the original's move. A **holdout** is a set of board positions excluded from fitting. [Opponent and position definitions](PHASE_V.md#positions-opponents-and-uncertainty)

## Wins and copying on the same cases

Eight originals per game were used to choose among 56 VNM rules and tune the selected rule. Confirmation used the remaining 24 tic-tac-toe, 16 Connect Four and 16 Reversi originals. Each original supplied two independently randomized board partitions and game collections, with two independently initialized fits per partition. This review replayed both selection rules for both networks, verified their individual external results and aggregate move agreement against the confirmation archive, and played the original on the same external cases. The additional original and move-removal comparisons are checkpoint analyses of these same originals. [Selection and precision plan](experiments/win_selection/DESIGN.md) · [Replay](scripts/review_checkpoint_f.py)

Entries are percentages, **mean ± sample standard deviation** across independent originals after averaging their four fits. The equal-game row gives one third to each named game. Its spread describes the average of one independent original drawn from each game. Tic-tac-toe used exact game probabilities from the empty board against all four groups, including five untrained networks. Each larger game used 4,096 games per method (16 originals × four fits × 32 opening/opponent pairs × both seats). Half the starts were the ordinary opening, half were random legal prefixes up to six turns. Within an original and a fit, methods share the boards, seats, opponents, openings and random numbers. ## What the scores explain

To test a prediction about the original's preferences, remove its favorite move at the current decision, ask which remaining move its frozen network scores highest, and compare this answer with each reconstruction. Only the current choice changes. For this comparison we required **at least two moves still available after removal**, so a forced answer cannot add an automatic match. The holdouts supplied 559–907 such tic-tac-toe positions, 159–160 Connect Four positions and 36–60 Reversi positions per partition. Across partitions the full agreement comparison used 729–1,091, 160 and 184 positions respectively. Tic-tac-toe and Connect Four weight the evaluated boards equally. Reversi weights sampled boards by the prevalence of positions with several placements, one placement or a forced pass among reachable withheld positions, then conditions on the stated population. The observations preserve these weights and all original and predicted choices. [Board-by-board observations](experiments/checkpoint_f/observations.jsonl.gz)

With both networks and both selection rules, the highest equal-game average for this particular question came from direct Q, **65.71 ± 5.58%**. The VNM Q result was **45.34 ± 2.66%**, a paired change of **−20.37 ± 5.96 points** [−23.48, −17.25], adjusted p = 7.07 × 10⁻¹⁰. These predictions measure an observable change in behavior. To identify an internal objective would require evidence distinguishing different objectives that produce the same choices. [Q comparisons and alternative explanations](experiments/checkpoint_f/summary.json) · [Other predictors with different access to the original](PHASE_V.md#copying-winning-and-explaining-changed-moves)

**Which parts helped win?** With the same fitted V and same whole-game scoring rule, allowing four simulated opponent types in place of one guarded opponent gained **8.68 ± 3.21 points** in wins [7.08, 10.29], adjusted p = 7.12 × 10⁻¹¹. Holding the continuation and supplied game scores fixed, adding the explicit learned frequency term gained **0.58 ± 1.30 points** [−0.05, 1.21], adjusted p = 1.00. Both alternatives still use learned scores to choose later moves. These estimates come from the earlier, separately adjusted confirmation comparisons across 24/16/16 originals. They support keeping the win-seeking standard and a comparison whose whole-game reward comes entirely from visits. [Component comparisons and exact-copy games](experiments/win_selection/README.md#which-choices-mattered)

**How much did fitting contribute?** The fitting label is the number of times the original chose a move divided by all visits to the preceding board. Each legal training candidate has equal weight. Tic-tac-toe minimized summed squared prediction errors with a penalty on squared non-intercept output weights. Both larger games minimized half the sum of squared prediction errors. At the reported fits the mean squared error (squared frequency units) was 0.1266 ± 0.0043, 0.1132 ± 0.0037 and 0.1233 ± 0.0097 for V, and 0.1038 ± 0.0037, 0.0540 ± 0.0069 and 0.0772 ± 0.0200 for Q, in game order (24/16/16 originals). The perfect-fit control set each observed favorite's score to one and the other moves to zero and covered continuations after every alternative. Direct selection then copied every tested training choice. Under copies continuing by direct play, adding all signed scores with a discount strictly between zero and one preserves the favorite. Counting only the last signed frequency instead copied 64.77 ± 5.56%, 23.52 ± 9.34% and 87.65 ± 0.66% (32/24/24 originals). Fit, coverage and selection each affect the conclusion.
