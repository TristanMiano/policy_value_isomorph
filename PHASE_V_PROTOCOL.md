# Three-game comparison · prospective design

The original player chooses moves. A reconstruction learns from its observed legal moves. Direct selection chooses the largest fitted move frequency. Von Neumann–Morgenstern (VNM) selection tries each move, finishes the game and averages one specified numerical score of the entire play. Use the archived tic-tac-toe design and retain its independent original players. This document fixes the following two new studies before evaluating their networks.

## Independent units and observations

Use 24 independently initialized original networks per new game, two fresh collections and symmetry-group partitions per original, and two independently initialized recovery fits per partition. Within a game use each original's four fits as one statistical unit. In Connect Four alternate targets across the 24 originals: one teacher takes an immediate win, then blocks an immediate loss, then uses a randomly ordered preference among columns. The other always uses a randomly ordered column preference. Train the originals from legal random-prefix boards and freeze the networks' actual choices. In 4×4 Reversi the teacher takes the first row-order placement preserving the best guaranteed final outcome, and passes when required. Enumerate the reachable graph and train on that teacher. Fit shared weights first, then learn an additional board-specific output correction on the enumerated teacher data to reproduce its rule. Report both stages. The final Reversi originals share the same preferred moves; initialization can vary the ordering of their other moves. In both games also study the player taking the frozen favorite with probability ¾ and otherwise playing uniformly. Give source strength from both seats before comparisons.

For each partition generate 384 legal random-prefix starting positions and include 96 games starting at the ordinary opening. For varied starts play one complete game per start for fixed originals and three for randomized ones. Both seats use the original. Count every transition and its parent visits. Assign boards and their reflected counterparts to fitting, validation (choice of settings), or evaluation by a fresh randomized group hash, approximately 65/15/20. Reserve the natural opening for evaluation. Keep successor inputs shared with evaluation and validation out of V training. Include an independent evaluation pool of 1,200 random-prefix starts in Connect Four, sampling up to 160 distinct withheld boards; report its distribution as sampled reachable positions. In Reversi sample up to 128 multiple-placement, 32 single-placement and 24 pass boards from the enumerated withheld positions, weighting each stratum in proportion to its withheld population. Evaluate additionally on withheld decisions from 32 original self-play trajectories per partition. Record positions reached during reconstruction play and unusually difficult or rare positions separately.

Before reconstructions play, evaluate the original against the panel. Give equal weight to uniform legal moves, two independently initialized untrained networks that take their highest-scoring legal move, a positional rule and a tactical rule. The positional rule favors central columns in Connect Four and corners in Reversi; the tactical rule takes a win, blocks a win then prefers the center in Connect Four, and preserves its best guaranteed outcome in Reversi. Fixed ties use the smallest legal column or row-order placement. Use 32 matched opening pairs per fit, eight per opponent group, half from the natural opening and half from legal random prefixes of up to six turns. Play both seats. Evaluate each original on the same openings before scoring its fits. Play each VNM rule against its own direct comparator with swapped seats using the same starts. Record individual outcomes, source, opening, opponent, fit and seed. Random draws for actual moves use a common uniform number at the same turn of the same game across comparisons. Keep draws for internal plans separate.

## Selection and additional questions

Fit a network scoring only the resulting board (V) and one scoring the current board and move (Q) to move counts divided by parent visits. Use squared frequency error, with equal weight for legal training candidates. Fit two representations of board patterns (local groups and a second grouping crossing them) on the same split, two starts for each, and record fitting curves, losses and examples at the actual visit count. Primary comparisons use local patterns. Additional controls are a PyTorch move imitator fitted on the same observed counts, a model fitted to the original network's legal scores with access labeled, a simple move-frequency ordering, a ranking from restricted-choice questions, and complete-game outcomes with supplied game points (win +1, draw 0, loss −1) or one draw value learned from choices. Disclose access and number of adjustable numbers. Choose settings on validation positions. A complete-play score for learned VNM uses the last move's fitted frequency, the sum of frequencies, or a sum giving each subsequent move 0.9 times its predecessor's weight. Sign each move for its actual mover, positive for first player, negative for second, and take the mover's best expected score. Forced passes count as a turn. For each utility compare both seats continuing by the fitted direct rule versus the original; use one exact deterministic continuation or four sampled randomized continuations per candidate. Recognize Monte Carlo variation in the latter. The primary pair uses final-move utility and fitted direct copies. For randomized selection give all legal moves equal base weight and sample proportional to exp(score/temperature). Compare temperatures 0 (equal among tied maxima), 0.05, 0.25 and 1 and a validation-selected setting. Record probability error, diversity, agreement and games. Keep the directly fitted game-point control separate from visit targets.

Predict restricted moves and edits before obtaining the original answer. Compare removing its favorite, removing another move and swapping the top two priorities. On a game-consequence probe compare predictions of terminal wins/draws/losses under alternative continuations with actual simulated outcomes. A board player sees its board and legal menu; a separate lottery-response experiment requires an independently defined player with a lottery input. Compare active questions (positions with the largest between-fit disagreement) with random and visitation-prioritized questions at equal budgets of 8 and 24. Use fresh answers to evaluate how many predictions can be corrected from those questions. Retain raw results and the evaluation population. Build an illustrated or interactive atlas showing actions, source choices, move-score gaps, variation across fits, continuations and restricted moves.

## Precision and comparison across games

Design simulation uses 2,000 studies per scenario. On the fraction scale the assumed between-source spreads for agreement, win rate and game points are 0.085/0.12/0.21 for Connect Four and 0.07/0.11/0.19 for Reversi, with additional partition and fit variation. Use 24 independent units in both games, targeting central individual interval half-widths near 0.05 agreement, 0.065 wins and 0.11 points. Repeat according to the fixed budget regardless of direction. Analyze paired changes across originals, giving the mean, sample standard deviation, individual 95% Student intervals, whole-original resampling intervals, counts in each direction and numerical two-sided p-values. Adjust across the 24 primary comparisons (two networks, three measures, three games and their equally weighted average) using Holm's procedure. For the three-game average draw original units independently within each game, then weight each game one third. The target is these three games under their specified board and opponent distributions. Compare fixed originals primarily, and give separate comparisons for randomized originals using the fraction of probability that must be reassigned and the chance of matching an independent draw.
