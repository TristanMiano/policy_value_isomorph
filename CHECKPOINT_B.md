# Checkpoint B · Which claims and comparisons to carry forward

**Decision, 2026-11-20.** Keep the original question: how closely can scores recovered from a frozen player reproduce its choices, and what else do those scores explain? A **source policy** is the original rule for choosing moves or assigning them probabilities. A **decoder** turns scores into a move or distribution. Phase I supports using exact copying as a reference, measuring what the supplied game payoff explains, and separately fitting a shared utility from choices to predict new ones. The choice of a trained formulation stays open for each research aim. [Research stance](RESEARCH_STANCE.md) · [Claim ledger](CLAIM_LEDGER.md)

## What the review found

**Agreement can change for two different reasons, even with exact payoffs.** In the archived tic-tac-toe pilot the **priority player** takes an immediate win, blocks an immediate loss, takes the center, takes a corner, or takes any legal cell, in that order, breaking ties by lowest cell number. After forcing a candidate move, let that player play both seats until the end. A win for the mover is worth +1, a draw 0 and a loss −1. The **exact-return player** compares those final points and chooses the lowest-numbered move with the best return. A recount from the saved legal-move payoffs gave 3,432 identical decisions, 853 different decisions tied on payoff, and 235 decisions where the source chose a lower payoff, over all 4,520 reachable decision boards. The source gave up 242 points summed over those boards, or 0.05354 points per decision with equal board weights. Repeating the recount for a source that always takes the lowest-numbered move with the best outcome it can guarantee against perfect replies gave 4,520 identical decisions and 0 points given up. These are enumerations of fixed players. [Per-board records](experiments/tictactoe_pilot/results/boards.csv.gz) · [Pilot interpretation](TICTACTOE_PILOT.md#why-the-moves-differed)

The game ranking also depends on the question. The table uses complete games from the empty opening. Each evaluated player takes every turn in its own seat. Give equal weight to X and O and one quarter to each opponent group: uniform random legal play, five equally weighted untrained networks with fixed random weights choosing the highest-scoring legal cell, the priority player and the perfect-reply player just defined. Network ties go to the lowest cell. Probabilities were computed over all continuations, conditional on this fixed panel. **VNM** denotes von Neumann–Morgenstern expected utility, which evaluates a move by the probability-weighted mean of one declared utility of complete plays.

The return-based player **won 6.79 percentage points more often and lost 14.18 points more often**. Its average score fell by **0.07394 game points**. Differences use unrounded values. Report move agreement, win probability and expected points separately. [Per-opponent and per-seat outcomes](experiments/tictactoe_pilot/results/matchups.csv)

**Two ambiguities affected the design.** One note ranked direct imitation first because an exact encoding can copy a known policy. That construction does not rank models trained on a finite sample. The corrected comparison gives the same fitted weights to direct and expected-utility selection, tests both on the same withheld boards, and records any access to the source during selection. A second note used the same sign for a log score and an energy. If larger scores are preferred, the energy minimized by the same choice rule is the negative of that score. Reversing this sign during sampling would favor the opposite end of the ordering. [Corrected design](docs/policy_reconstruction_vs_utility_factorization.md) · [Notation](formalism/00_notation.md)

## Terminology and review record

Use **decoder score** for a number consumed by a selection rule and **revealed ordering** for a preference inferred from actual choices under changed conditions. A **return** is the accumulated payoff on one play. An **expected return** averages that quantity under the declared continuation. A **latent reward** is a proposed payoff rule inferred from behavior. A **mechanism** is a computation inside the source whose causal role is tested by intervention. A move-frequency score can enter a declared whole-play utility. An expected-utility score can in turn feed either maximization or an additional temperature sampler. Label the latter **VNM scores + temperature sampling** and give its temperature. These operations describe different steps in one calculation. **Self-review.** An unconditional claim for direct imitation would hide what a compact utility might predict on unseen boards. Calling a payoff-maximizing move the source's preference would hide both arbitrary tie choices and real payoff differences. The same audit therefore kept the exact-copy reference, checked the tied and lower-payoff cases separately, and retained fresh changed-choice predictions as the test of an explanation. The relative performance of independently fitted utilities on the three games remains an open empirical question.
