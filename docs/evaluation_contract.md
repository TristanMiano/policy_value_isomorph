# How to choose a formulation for each research aim

A **source policy** is the frozen player whose decisions are being studied. A **formulation** consists of a representation of scores, what the scores were fitted to, and the rule for turning them into moves. A **fit** is one trained instance. Use three separate rankings. Publish each formulation's measurements in all three wherever the necessary observations exist. Choose training settings on validation data (data set aside for development), lock the choices and report the rankings on fresh evaluation data. [Notation](../formalism/00_notation.md) · [Claim register](../CLAIM_LEDGER.md)

| Aim | Primary comparison and selection rule | What accompanies the ranking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fidelity to source moves | For a deterministic source that always takes one move, maximize the fraction of equally weighted held-out boards with the exact source move. For a stochastic source that samples moves, minimize mean total variation distance, half the sum of absolute legal-move probability differences at each board. Zero means identical distributions. | Give counts, distributions, ties and sources of probability error. Report source-visit-weighted results separately. |
| Playing strength | Maximize the evaluated player's expected game points against the fixed opponent panel below. | Also rank by win probability and give draws, losses, each seat, each opponent and head-to-head play. |
| Interpretability of source choices | Predict fresh source responses to changed menus and to changed outcome probabilities. Minimize prediction error on the two intervention families, giving each family half the weight when both can be observed. For a deterministic response use disagreement, and for a distribution use total variation. | Give family-specific errors, explanation size and across-fit variation. Among exact primary ties compare size within a common description unit, then variation. If units or available families differ, report the conditional rankings and the unresolved trade-off. |

Methods tied on the primary measurement share rank. Use unrounded values for ranking and distinguish numerical approximation from a known mathematical tie. Give all tied methods. For sampled results, a numerical lead is an estimate with uncertainty. Publish each relevant paired difference even when its interval crosses zero. Choosing a formulation for one aim never removes its results for the others.

## Inputs, splits and comparisons

A **board network (V)** scores the board resulting from a move. A **board-and-move network (Q)** scores a move in its original context. Train and freeze the source first. Preserve raw transition counts, parent visit totals, distinct observed boards, the starting-board rule and the opponent for each collection. The target for a visited parent is the count for each move divided by that parent's total observed source turns. Fit only on allowed boards, document the loss actually optimized, its units and weighting, and report the measured loss at the published fit. Compare the effects of fit error and unseen positions separately. For remaining disagreements show legal-move predictions multiplied by the same observed visit total as the actual counts, and label fitted versus withheld examples. A network trained on game outcomes is a different data condition.

For each fit, compare direct selection with **von Neumann–Morgenstern (VNM) expected-utility selection**, which scores a candidate by the mean of one learned utility of complete games after forcing that first move. Compare largest-score choice and sampling proportional to exponentiated scores at a specified temperature (the score scale governing randomness). Label every VNM row with the utility, time discount, perspective, continuation policy for each seat and simulations per candidate. Report source calls and simulator calls at selection, including any source used for continuation. Group independent and source-assisted deployment by access. Also report the original player, exact observed-frequency references where counts exist, an exact source-distribution reference where probabilities are available, and a supplied-payoff reference. Evaluate all candidates on identical eligible cases and give coverage when a method cannot score some cases.

For the existing tic-tac-toe split, there are 2,980 fitting, 760 validation and 780 evaluation decision boards. Keep rotations and reflections in the same group. Report distinct boards and their weights. The 780 have been inspected in earlier work and serve as an exploratory comparison. For confirmation, fix a new split or fresh independent sources and recorded seeds before fitting, publish the split and separate examples observed during development from those newly withheld. For larger games, define the legal starting distribution, how rare states are sampled and the target weighting before play. Counts deliberately balanced over starting positions do not estimate natural visitation without reweighting. ## Fidelity and score structure

Give the two populations separately. The first weights each withheld decision board once. The second weights boards by expected visits under the original source playing both seats from the empty opening, renormalizing over eligible withheld boards and reporting the share of source visits retained. If that share is zero the weighted estimate is undefined. For a stochastic reconstruction of a deterministic source, exact agreement in expectation at a board is the probability assigned to its chosen move. Comparing highest-probability moves is an additional statistic. For a stochastic source retain the distributional comparison even when its most likely move matches.

For every board record the number of legal moves and moves tied for best. Report how often the original move lies in the best set, how often the declared tie rule selects it, and the gap between its score and the mover's best. Also give the gap between the two best legal scores, defined as zero on a tied top and inapplicable on a forced move. Orient scores so larger favors the mover and state units before pooling. **Cross-entropy** is the mean negative natural logarithm of the reconstructed probability of a source draw, in nats per move. If the reconstruction assigns zero to a positive-probability source move, give the affected board count and missing source probability mass and report infinite cross-entropy. A zero empirical count alone does not establish zero source probability.

Report pairwise ordering reversals on common legal move pairs with both ties and pair counts. Compare score magnitudes only after fixing their origin, scale and temperature. Compare changes in normalized move gaps across neighboring boards under a stated legal relationship and matching of moves, for example the same still-legal cell before and after one move, expressed in a common perspective. Give the eligible pair count. Relate these errors to changed moves, lost game points and intervention error by reporting each against the source gap and visit count. Exact boardwise ties retain their observed frequency.

## Playing strength

For tic-tac-toe start each game empty. Give each seat half the weight and each of four opponent groups one quarter. The first selects uniformly among legal cells. The second comprises five fixed randomly initialized networks, each taking its highest-scoring legal cell, equally weighted within the group. The third wins immediately, blocks a win, takes the center, takes a corner or takes any legal cell, in that order. The fourth takes the move with the best guaranteed outcome against perfect replies. Ties go to the lowest legal cell for the last three groups. Use the saved opponent identities or independently frozen replacements specified before a new comparison. For a new game predeclare the analogous panel and weights before evaluating methods. [Opponent identities and weighting](../experiments/tictactoe_pilot/manifest.json)

Deploy the reconstruction on **every turn in its seat**. From that player's perspective, one win earns +1, one draw 0 and one loss −1, so expected points per game equal win probability minus loss probability. Publish game counts or exact probability mass, plus the paired difference from the original player's result for the same opponent and seat. Also play the source and reconstruction against each other in both seats. Enumerate where feasible. For random simulations match openings, seats, opponents and chance draws where meaningful. State which random choices are shared. The return when the source plays both seats *after one forced move* answers a separate question. A policy that trades wins and losses for draws can rank differently by points and by win probability.

## Interpretability and uncertainty

Reserve whole board groups and interventions before fitting. For menu removal, compare the original menu with a specified legal subset, including cases with the original first choice removed and a control removing another move. Obtain a fresh source response under the new menu. For changed outcome probabilities, use matched options and explicitly alter the lottery of consequences presented to the source. Record what the player can observe, how both seats continue, and its actual response. An existing network accepting only the unchanged board cannot provide a response to an unrepresented lottery change. Mark that intervention unavailable for the source, report coverage and restrict comparisons to matched information. Silent masking or altered rollouts can supply model predictions, not independent source labels.

Compare each explanation with (1) the source network's raw legal move scores, converted by its original selection rule, (2) a model fitted to imitate the same observed choices and (3) a simple explicit rule fitted on the same information, such as one ranking of cells by observed choice frequency, with lowest-cell ties. Report the additional model access used to obtain raw scores. Withhold all intervention labels used for the final comparison, including from setting the temperature. Count stored scalar parameters, lookup entries or decision clauses, naming units, inputs and shared preprocessing. Report errors for each size and variation of predictions over independent fits. A formulation is dominated for these purposes if another has no greater error, description size and variation and improves at least one on the same cases and units. Publish the remaining trade-offs. Human understanding requires measurements from people, and a claim about internal cause requires interventions on the model itself.

Pair all differences on the same boards, opponents, seats, collections and training initializations where applicable. For an exact finite enumeration give the population difference directly. For sampled games give game or opening-cluster counts and paired uncertainty. For repeated fits give each paired difference and its standard error, the standard deviation of the differences divided by the square root of the number of independent pairs. If estimating uncertainty over boards, resample whole rotation/reflection groups together. For repeated moves in sampled games resample whole games. Propagate uncertainty from estimating source probabilities independently of reconstruction fits, using independent draws or resampling the original collections. Exact outcomes conditional on one fitted network still vary across fits. State which sources of randomness an interval covers.

**Self-review.** Using only ordinary boards favors an accurate imitator even if its predictions fail when a preferred move disappears. Ranking only by wins favors risk-taking policies where draws matter. Fresh intervention labels and separate points and wins rankings preserve both effects. The strength of an interpretability claim still depends on which changes the original player can actually experience.
