# Independent tic-tac-toe comparisons · Task 62

The **source** is the original player. A **fit** is one trained reconstruction. **Direct** selects the largest immediate score. **Von Neumann–Morgenstern (VNM) selection** tries each candidate and maximizes the average utility of a complete play from the mover's perspective. This protocol fixes the following design before fitting or evaluating the confirmation players.

## Population and budget

Use 32 independent source blocks, numbered 0–31. In each, draw one random ordering of the nine cells. Train an independently initialized neural player to choose a move guaranteeing the best win/draw/loss outcome against best replies, breaking ties by that ordering. Use all 4,520 reachable decisions, ten cell/side inputs, two 128-unit layers retaining positive activations, nine outputs and negative log probability of the teacher move. Use shuffled batches of 256, Adam (updates scaled by past gradients), initial step 0.003 and the established reductions after passes 100 and 200. Check every ten passes until exact imitation, continuing at the final step size if needed. Report every block, training duration, move agreement with its own ordering, guaranteed payoff given up and agreement with lowest-cell optimal play. Freeze before collecting visits. From the same original player form a randomized source selecting its preferred move with probability 3/4, otherwise uniformly among legal cells. Also study a restricted-support version drawing only among the top two eligible choices, and a menu-dependent fixture that reverses its ordering on pairs. A fixture is an explicit rule to which a fresh question can be posed.

For block b use source initialization 6201+b and independent random streams derived from (6221,b,purpose,split,fit). Use two independently randomized holdouts per block. Put board rotations and reflections together, assign approximately 65%, 15%, 20% of symmetry groups to fit, validation (select settings), and final evaluation. Always reserve the opening for evaluation. Two independent reconstruction initializations per holdout give 128 paired fits per source class and network type. Measure every withheld board and enumerate full games from the opening, each seat half weight. Give a quarter each to uniform legal moves, five fresh untrained networks selecting their largest legal output, an immediate win/block/center/corner/any priority rule, and lowest-cell optimal play. Fixed rules use the lowest available cell to resolve ties unless otherwise specified. Report each seat and opponent, the original player, head-to-head games and best responses with access to each opponent. Rotate no external game into this phase.

Collect complete games by starting at each decision in its own partition, with two source copies finishing both sides. Count only parent boards in the starting partition, retain raw counts and denominators. Use one start per board for fixed choices and eight for the randomized source. Additional coverage comparison per block and source uses empty self-play, empty against uniform, mixed random legal prefixes, and least visited of 16 such candidates. Use the established mixed schedule, a pool of 512 generated openings, and 512 and 2,048 observed source moves. Save the generation cost and extra moves to finish the last game. Evaluate covered, common and rare boards against exact visitation under self-play and uniform opposition. In the frequency experiment targets are chosen moves divided by visits; each eligible legal candidate has equal fitting weight. Withhold shared successor inputs for the resulting-board network. Report losses and disagreements in predicted counts at actual visits.

## Methods, information and controls

The primary reconstruction uses a neural network with a random hidden layer of 64 or 192 rectified units and a trained linear output. For the board network (V) give the resulting cells, side and observable line geometry. For the board-and-move network (Q) give both board descriptions and the candidate cell indicators. Scale inputs using training examples only. Fit the output to the frequency target with squared error and a squared-weight penalty; compare penalty strengths 0.01, 1 and 100 (sum-of-squares units) on identical features. Keep the width and penalty with smallest validation prediction error. Bound predicted frequencies to [0,1] before selection and report raw residual and clipping. Keep per-setting training and validation errors and held-out agreement and points. A half-training-data control keeps the same hidden features. Include a fully trainable Q imitation control using the source network architecture trained on sampled moves at training boards for 30 passes (Adam, step 0.003, batch 256). Use no source calls at deployment for either direct model.

Primary VNM uses the fitted frequency on the final move, positive when X moved, negative when O moved, with copies of the fitted direct player finishing both seats. Compare the same final utility with original-player continuation, the signed sum of moves, and the sum discounting each next move by 0.9, using each continuation rule. Force every first move, continue to the end, prefer larger X utility for X and smaller for O, break ties at the lowest cell. One continuation is exact for a fixed player. For randomized continuation use 16 sampled continuations per candidate and compare 1, 4 and 16 for the game-payoff reference. Distinguish learned utilities from supplied game points (+1 X win, 0 draw, −1 O win). Fit game-point and outcome-probability predictors from the same training rollouts as additional references. Report independent move draws and probability distributions for randomized players. Use exact complete source probabilities and exact specified continuations as access-labeled references.

For restricted menus, compare repeated removal, actively chosen pairs and uniformly shuffled pairs with budgets of 1, 3 and 8 queries on 32 held-out multi-move boards per block. Compare determined order, unresolved pairs and contradictions to the specified rule. For known stochastic laws compare exact log probabilities, empirical frequencies from 1, 4, 16 and 64 draws, and unit pseudocount smoothing (one added count per legal move). Compare probability distance (half the sum of absolute probability differences), missing support and source-weighted log probability ratios, retaining infinity for assigning zero to a possible source move. For soft selection, sample legal moves with weights proportional to exp(score / temperature) at temperatures 0.05, 0.25, 1, the low-temperature equal-best limit, plus temperature selected on validation. Equal legal base weights, explicit support masks. Compare a draw mixture of utilities, a tie mixture, outcome distribution prediction and direct utility prediction. Measure signed score residuals after removing a board-specific offset.

For compact utilities assign X's win +1, loss −1 and draw a fitted value on the grid −1, −0.75, …, 1. Use training choices and common lotteries (probability distributions over endings under the given continuation) for all candidates. Select by training move matches and resolve tied fits toward zero then lowest value. Report equivalent grid choices and held-out errors. Before obtaining fresh answers, predict choices after the preferred move is removed, when another move is removed, and when a specified edit reverses a cell priority. Compare network raw outputs, visit and game scores, utility and a fixed ordering learned from training counts. Use separate lottery-choice fixtures for the sure draw against a varying chance of win or loss. Include a known utility and a rule depending on presentation order that cannot be captured by one stable utility. Freeze predictions before reading the new responses. An ordinary board network has no interface for changed outcome lotteries. Report coverage per intervention and condition rankings on matched access.

## Estimation and stopping

The three aims are copying decisions (highest agreement on equally weighted withheld boards, lowest probability distance for randomized players), game strength (highest mean points against the common panel), and predicting new choices (lowest error, equal weight to removed-move and changed-lottery families where both are observed). Give absolute wins, draws and losses and visitation-weighted fidelity separately. Report competing rankings and per-fit observations. For the primary family use the two network types × agreement, win probability and mean points for the fixed optimal player, comparing VNM minus direct on identical fitted weights. Average fits and partitions within each source block. Across the 32 independent blocks report mean, sample standard deviation, paired individual 95% Student intervals, two-sided p-values for zero mean change and Holm adjustment across six tests. Supplement with whole-block resampling and the share of positive, zero and negative block differences. This averages over random splits of one fixed finite game population. Reuse of boards across blocks is part of the target; it cannot support inference to new games. Count sources, partitions, fits, openings and both seats without treating their Cartesian product as independent. Other comparisons are exploratory, use explicit families and identify exact finite quantities.

Before source collection, 4,000 simulated studies per design compared 12, 24 and 32 source blocks. Two holdouts and two fits per holdout separate variation within and between sources. The simulation reuses a finite pool of 627 board groups, resamples approximately 125 per test set, and adds independent source, split and initialization variation. The central assumed standard deviations are 6, 5 and 8 percentage points, scaled together by 0.6 and 1.4 for sensitivity. We target individual interval half-widths of approximately 3 percentage points and 0.06 game points centrally. At 32 blocks the simulated means are 2.89 points and 0.058 game points (sensitivity 1.77–4.03 percentage points). Power, the chance a test detects a stipulated change, for changes of 2, 5 and 8 points centrally is 0.096, 0.750 and 0.996 under a conservative adjustment for six comparisons. Run the fixed budget irrespective of observed direction. Save raw results, per-fit differences, uncertainties, precision achieved and limitations.
