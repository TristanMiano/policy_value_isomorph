# Isolating changes in move agreement · 23 November 2026

This is a follow-up to published Phase V results, using all of its fixed-choice originals and archived independent splits and fits. Each comparison reuses the same openings, opponents, board weights and frozen networks. The earlier precision design governs the 32 tic-tac-toe and 24+24 larger-game source blocks. This is a diagnosis on previously studied populations. Separate confirmation on new populations remains open.

**Original** means the frozen player's favorite legal move. **V** predicts move frequency from the resulting board, **Q** from the board and proposed move. **Direct** chooses the largest immediate score. **Von Neumann–Morgenstern (VNM) selection** chooses the largest expected utility, a numerical score for a complete game from the current mover's perspective. For each fixed original use exact deterministic paths and lowest-numbered legal ties.

1. Verify every positive visit count against the frozen favorite. Compare direct selection on fitting positions and withheld positions. Replace fitted scores by exact original move frequencies (1 for the chosen move, 0 otherwise), and separately by a resulting-board score that averages exact labels of all incoming legal moves. Give the population used for that average. Test the contribution of bounding individual predictions to [0,1] by comparing the same unbounded outputs. Classify incorrect moves as strict ordering reversals or ties resolved differently. Preserve board-level records.
2. Hold either fitted scores or exact source frequencies fixed while changing only who finishes both seats after a forced candidate, using the fitted direct rule or the original. Evaluate each resulting final-move VNM decision on the original boards. Report exact decision ties, strict reversals and changes of last mover. Distinguish the four combinations from changing both quantities at once. Record examples including the path, first and final scores. The exact-frequency intervention gives a privileged action-and-board diagnostic for either architecture.
3. Test a whole-game utility for copying. At every move subtract the highest local fitted score from the fitted score of the move actually made. Sum these signed differences over the complete game, positive sign for first-player terms and negative for second-player terms. Verify the identity with direct selection under two greedy copies, evaluate the rule when the original continues, and retain the final-move benchmark. Test absolute playing outcomes alongside agreement.
4. Refit V and Q in both larger games after adding complete original-player games from 4,096 varied training starts per partition, using the same original fitting settings, validation boards, initialization seed and reserved inputs. Use different reachable Reversi decision boards and legally generated Connect Four prefixes. Count additional observed boards and moves. Tic-tac-toe already collected from every board, so use its archived half-versus-full coverage control. Independently test a move-frequency model trained on the same original observations with an action preference and optional consequence features. For tic-tac-toe and Reversi use the game rules to supply the outcome guaranteed against perfect replies. For Connect Four use immediate win and blocking features. Learn all coefficients from visits. Include an action-only fit with the identical training objective as an information control. Report separate game-rule access and targeted choice of features for these particular teachers.
5. Average four paired fits per original before inference. Report mean and sample standard deviation across originals, source-level paired differences, individual 95% Student intervals, two-sided p-values against zero mean difference and Holm adjustment across the reported diagnostic contrast family. Retain exact equalities as such. Preserve any observed deterioration. No inference of unique hidden motives or of a universal advantage for planning follows from these interventions.

## Perfect training-agreement control requested after the diagnosis began

Q stores an indicator for every seen board and candidate. Its trained weights equal the observed fractions. For the two larger games also collect complete original continuations starting after *each* legal move of *each* original training board. Only moves freely chosen by the original enter counts. Add these observed labels to Q and compare direct and final-move VNM on exactly the original training positions. Verify direct agreement, mean squared training error, and whether every candidate path uses fitted positions throughout. This additional collection isolates selection even when an alternative would have taken the baseline fit outside its experience. Tic-tac-toe already covers the entire reachable decision population.
