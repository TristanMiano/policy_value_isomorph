# Refinement after the first occupancy-only comparison

After observing that the first occupancy-only choice won fewer games than its matched constant-score control, test two explanations. Eight sampled paths may give a noisy estimate of a move's expected score. The fitted frequency may also put too much or too little weight on the identity of the terminal move compared with who took it. Use fresh external neural opponents, openings, match randomness and planning paths for this follow-up. Previously trained originals and position partitions are reused. This is a new panel conditional on the archived originals, rather than a new original population.

Cross both V and Q, uniform play by both seats and greedy own play against the equal mixture of greedy and uniform replies, and 8, 32 and 64 uniform paths per stochastic component. Compare the signed final frequency at time discounts 0.75, 0.9 and 0.97; also compare the square root and the square of the raw final frequency, both discounted by 0.9. These transforms are zero at zero and one at one, introduce no terminal bonus, and preserve the ordering of nonnegative predictions. The square root compresses relative differences and the square magnifies them. All 60 configurations exclude supplied game results and hand-written tactics. Try them on the eight discovery originals per game. Freeze the leading equal-game mean before opening the new confirmation panels for the remaining 24/16/16 originals. Retain the prior 8-path incumbent, runner-up, both architectures, best by game and transform, matched raw-frequency and budget comparisons, signed and unsigned sums, direct selection, original, historical references, and the matched constant controls. Use the same precision plan, repetitions, reporting and path measurements as the [first search](DESIGN.md). Round mean candidate utilities to 12 decimal places solely to resolve numerical ties consistently in both implementations.
