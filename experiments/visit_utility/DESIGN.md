# Choosing completed-game scores from observed moves alone

23 November 2026, before running this comparison. A frozen original chooses its highest-scoring legal move. A reconstruction learns each move's count divided by visits to its parent. V predicts that frequency from the resulting board and Q from the board and action. The question is how often they can win when their fitted frequencies supply every score in planning. Von Neumann–Morgenstern (VNM) selection chooses the move with the highest expected numerical score of a complete game. Higher scores on won games, the effect of game length, and the win rate of the resulting player are distinct measurements.

Use exact expectations in tic-tac-toe and eight random paths per sampled component in the other games. The deterministic component is exact. For discovery compare both networks, all four continuations, and each of these 11 scores:

- The fitted frequency of the final move, either positive for the chooser's move and negative for the opponent's (signed), or always positive (unsigned).
- The sum of all signed moves and the sum of all unsigned moves. For each, try successive weights with ratios 1, 0.9 or 0.5, beginning with weight 1.
- The signed final frequency discounted by 0.9 for each preceding turn, the sum of the chooser's own frequencies discounted by 0.9 each turn, or the signed final frequency plus 0.1 times the signed 0.9-discounted sum.
