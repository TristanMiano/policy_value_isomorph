# Choosing the VNM rule by games won

23 November 2026. This follow-up concerns the previously studied fixed-choice players. The broader question is which learned trajectory utilities help a frozen reconstruction win games. Our proposed explanation is that counting the last mover discards game consequences, and that projecting one peer's replies can misrank moves against a different opponent. A candidate that expects a loss against one projected reply and a win against another distinguishes the two explanations.

**Win rate** is the fraction of external games won, averaging the two seats and the four Phase V opponent groups equally. **Direct selection** takes the largest fitted visit frequency. **Von Neumann–Morgenstern (VNM) selection** tries each legal action and chooses the greatest expected numerical score of the resulting whole play from the current player's perspective. The two seats thereafter follow explicitly named rules during planning. When actually playing, the method makes a fresh plan on each turn. The external opponent's identity, move probabilities and trained weights are unavailable during planning. Exact-copy self-play has both seats use the identical deployed method, including replanning. For seat-balanced copies, win and loss rates each equal half the share of decisive games. This measures draw propensity and first-seat advantage. It is reported separately from the selection criterion.

## Competing rules

Use the ordinary frozen visit-frequency fits from Phase IV/V, both the board-only model V and the board-and-move model Q. A fixed original picks its network's highest legal score. The repeat is one original with two independently randomized collections/board partitions and two initializations per partition. Both architectures and both selectors use identical cases. Count each of the three games equally. The standard is the deployable candidate with the highest discovery mean win rate across these three games. Also freeze the runner-up, the leading formulation whose entire utility is learned from visits, and a leading rule using both visits and outcomes. Record the best candidate in each game. Break exact cross-game ties by the order published by the runner. Evaluate and keep separately the forward-looking standard, its nearest competitors, pure outcome and pure learned controls, and changes of one component. Do not reselect from confirmation results.

Compare these six trajectory utilities plus one intermediate draw valuation. Final frequency counts only the frequency of the final move. Sum counts all move frequencies. Discounted sum multiplies successive terms by 1, 0.9, 0.81 and so on. These three terms alternate sign with the mover. Win-only counts 1 for the choosing player's win and 0 otherwise. Points counts 1 for a win, 0.5 for a draw and 0 for a loss, an affine rescaling of conventional zero-sum game points. Quarter-draw counts a draw as 0.25. The hybrid adds 0.20 times the discounted signed sum multiplied by 0.10 to quarter-draw utility. This bounds the absolute learned contribution by 0.20 for scores in [0,1]. Use the lowest legal index on ties. Include direct as a reference.

Use four deployable continuation pairs. **Copies** project direct fitted play for both seats. **Guarded copies** both favor an immediate win, avoid an immediate loss and minimize winning replies available to the opponent, then favor the fitted score. In Reversi they additionally prefer a corner, fewer immediately accessible opponent corners and fewer opponent placements, in that order. **Direct against a mixture** projects direct future moves for the choosing seat. **Guarded against a mixture** projects guarded future moves for that seat. The opponent is drawn once per hypothetical continuation, equally from direct, guarded, positional and uniform play. Positional chooses the center then corners in tic-tac-toe, central columns in Connect Four, and corners then edges in Reversi, with lowest-index ties. Uniform chooses a legal move uniformly. For the new games integrate the three deterministic opponents and average four random reply paths for the uniform quarter. Hash each board and replicate to draw a legal move independently of evaluation randomness and without using the true opponent. In tic-tac-toe sum the uniform probabilities exactly. Additionally compare an original playing both rollout seats as a privileged diagnostic. Compare changes in simulation budget and tie preference for the chosen rule on confirmation. Neither a game solver nor the frozen original is available to deployable planners.
