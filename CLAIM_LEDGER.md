# Claims, evidence and open obligations

Each identifier stays attached to one question as its evidence changes. **PV-H** denotes a policy–value hypothesis family. Letters distinguish individual claims. A **policy** gives a move or distribution over available moves. A **decoder** turns numerical scores into behavior. A **contract** records the observations, rules, information at deployment and evaluated population. A **construction** exhibits an example, a **proof obligation** names a result to establish under explicit assumptions, and an **empirical prediction** specifies a magnitude to measure. Predictions below are research expectations, never acceptance criteria. [Research stance](RESEARCH_STANCE.md) · [Roadmap and task numbers](TODO.md)

## Existing evidence and its scope

- The original pilot fixed the population, both seats and the opponents before measuring. One reference player chooses the lowest-numbered move giving the best outcome against perfect replies. Another wins immediately, blocks a win, takes the center, takes a corner or takes any legal cell, using the lowest eligible number. Choosing the move giving the best game payoff when the source plays both sides thereafter matched the first player on 4,520/4,520 boards and the second on 3,432/4,520, a difference of 1,088 decisions (24.07 percentage points). These exact counts use one vote per reachable decision board. [Pilot checkpoint and definitions](TICTACTOE_PILOT.md)
- In the later visit-count study, a neural player chose the first reference player's move on all 4,520 boards. A score per resulting board inferred from the full choice table reproduced all 4,520 choices with seven score levels. The same resulting board had incompatible observed frequency labels across parents on 2,026/5,477 resulting boards. Reproducing the order needed for a choice and fitting its observed frequency are different obligations. [Constructions and measured limits](TICTACTOE_CHOICE_TRAINING.md)
- Checkpoint A fixed the public calls and the meaning of signed payoff. Reversing whose win is positive and reversing the selection direction changed 0/4,520 choices for the win/block/center/corner player. The pilot and Checkpoint A remain the records for those decisions. [Pilot decision](TODO.md#checkpoint-before-the-pytorch-migration) · [Checkpoint A](CHECKPOINT_A.md)

Checkpoint B fixes the proof obligations for representation, equivalence and utility, and the minimum game experiments for imitation, strength and predictions under changed conditions. Across 4,520 decision boards the priority player's 1,088 disagreements with exact game-payoff selection split into 853 equally good moves and 235 lower-return moves. A return-based explanation depends on the payoff, continuation and resolution of ties. A claim about the internal cause still requires interventions on the source. [Review and disposition of all seven claim families](CHECKPOINT_B.md)

## Claim register

For the empirical entries, **agreement** is the fraction choosing the same move, higher meaning closer imitation. **Probability distance** is half the sum of absolute legal-move probability differences, averaged across boards, with zero meaning identical choices. **Gap** is the best score minus the next best in the mover's units. A **paired difference** subtracts two measurements on the same boards, seats or fits. Report variation over independent fits and the unit resampled. Knowable exact population counts carry no sampling error. Entries with an unknown effect distinguish a design reference or theoretical range from a measured magnitude.

| PV-H2a | Scores observed through a fixed decoder and query family | Prove that equality of responses defines equivalence classes. | PV-H2c | Choices from restricted sets of moves, under a stable strict ordering | Prove a recovery construction using pair queries and exhibit context-dependent reversals. Task 43. A full-menu response alone leaves comparisons between unchosen moves undetermined. |
| PV-H2d | A compact utility inferred from choices, using H1e and H2a | Give the same complete play the same score wherever it appears. Report the signed improvement, including reversals, plus utility size and access. Tasks 62–72. |
| PV-H3a | Repeated fits on the same split, using H2b | Measure paired move differences and normalized legal-pair order reversals across fits. For the optimal neural source, adding board geometry and modeling tie choice changed agreement by +12.74 ± 0.68 percentage points on 780 withheld boards for the board-and-move network (one standard error across three paired fits). Repeat on new splits and report how much of that gain recurs. [Study](TICTACTOE_CHOICE_TRAINING.md) |
| PV-H3b | Sample variation at rare boards, using H1b | Given independent observations of a move with probability p on n visits, the predicted standard deviation of its observed fraction is √(p(1−p)/n), halving at four times the visits. Compare by visit count, starting-board rule and independent game collections. Record model error separately from count variation. [Sampling study](TICTACTOE_SAMPLING.md) |
| PV-H3c | Geometry, meaning score changes between neighboring legal boards, using H2b | Compare normalized gaps and neighboring-board differences across independent fits with a shuffled-board reference preserving the score distribution. Expect smaller discrepancies between fits if they share local structure. Report the fraction of the shuffled discrepancy removed with its interval and raw differences if the reference is zero. Its magnitude outside the tic-tac-toe pilot is open. |
| PV-H4a | Predictions after removing a move or changing outcome probabilities, using H2c–d | Freeze the explanation before obtaining the new response. Expect any predictive advantage for recovered rankings to be largest when the original top move is removed. Report changes in probability distance on its 0–1 scale relative to raw scores, an imitator and a simple rule. Identical reference predictions imply an expected paired difference of zero on those cases. |
| PV-H4b | Explanation size at a given prediction error, using H4a | Count adjustable numbers, rule clauses or recorded scores and identify the unit. The seven-level construction gives a representational example, while its lookup stores an entry per resulting board. Compare errors and sizes together. Prospective benefit ranges from zero saved entries for a copy to the lookup size minus the shared representation size. Test predictive savings on new interventions. |
| PV-H5a | Exponential sampling from scores, using H1b and H2b | Prove dependence on score differences divided by temperature and on the base weights. Give the low-temperature limit for unique and tied maxima and positive mass assumptions. Task 42 and the stochastic-completion tasks. This defines a family compatible with one deterministic choice. |
| PV-H5b | Diversity of sampled moves after choosing units, using H5a | With two alternatives, equal base weights and a gap of one in temperature units, the lower move has probability 1/(1+e), about 0.269. Doubling temperature predicts about 0.378. Across boards measure probability distance, number of different moves and states per game budget, and game outcomes over independent runs. A new sampling rule alone supplies no observations of the original player's stochastic preferences. |
| PV-H6a | One contract across a board game, continuous controls and sequences, using H1a–c | Give typed examples and enumerate supported observations, candidate coverage and approximations per domain. A missed candidate of source probability mass m imposes probability distance at least m on every reconstructed distribution confined to the other candidates. Compare the observed missing mass with the measured distance. |
| PV-H6b | Recovery under equal access and evaluation budget, using H6a | Count queries, simulator steps and stored numbers alongside agreement or continuous action error in domain units. A finite deterministic complete table uses one source choice per context. Compare its cost with repeated or shared fits and report savings as the table count minus actual queries. No common effect size is assumed across domains. |
| PV-H7a | Predicting an external score from a neural player's hidden activity, using H3a–c | Fit a predictor on training boards and evaluate normalized squared error on withheld boards against shuffled labels and raw outputs. For labels independently shuffled within strata, the expected improvement over the stratum mean on fresh independent labels is zero in the population optimum. Estimate real-label improvement and paired uncertainty. Prediction measures association. |
| PV-H7b | Changing hidden activity while holding the board fixed, using H7a | Alter activity along the proposed score direction and compare with equally sized control changes. Predict an increased target-move probability for a positive aligned change if this direction contributes to choice. A control gives the reference difference zero under matched exchangeability. Measure signed shifts on the 0–1 scale, dose and effects on other moves. This tests causal involvement. |
| PV-H7c | Changing the training objective, using H7a–b | For a differentiable loss, negating it reverses its derivative exactly. Test how much the learned score alignment and intervention effect move when the objective itself changes. Compare with independent fits of the original objective. The predicted change from merely rewriting minimization as maximization is zero for identical updates. Historical and causal explanations of network generality remain open. |

**Self-review.** A perfect full-table fit can make the thesis appear stronger than evidence on new choices warrants. A poor fixed-payoff fit can make it appear weaker. H1a, H1e and H2d now have distinct data, comparisons and next obligations. One live alternative learns utility from common terminal consequences, another from time-weighted scores on complete paths. Changing the available continuations can distinguish them even when their opening choices coincide.
